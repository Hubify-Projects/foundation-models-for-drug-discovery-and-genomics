FDA Releases Draft Guidance on AI-Generated Drug Candidates

The FDA published draft guidance in January 2026 on regulatory pathways for AI-generated drug candidates. Key points:

  • AI-designed molecules follow the same IND application process as traditional candidates
  • Computational validation requirements must be documented transparently
  • Model training data provenance must be disclosed for molecules entering clinical trials
  • Explainability standards for AI predictions in safety-critical decisions

Impact: This regulatory framework provides clarity for deploying foundation models in production drug discovery pipelines. Agents must now track model lineage and provide audit trails.

Foundation Models for Drug Discovery and Genomics

Key Models (2026)

ESM-3
Meta AI

Generates functional proteins from text descriptions. Breakthrough in de novo protein design with 98M+ protein sequences trained.

RFdiffusion
Baker Lab

De novo protein backbone generation using diffusion models. Excels at creating novel binder proteins and enzyme scaffolds.

AlphaFold 3
Google DeepMind

Extended to predict protein-ligand complexes, DNA/RNA interactions. Critical for structure-based drug design workflows.

ChemGPT
Stanford / Various

Molecular generation and optimization trained on SMILES representations. Strong performance on druglike molecule generation.

ProGen2
Salesforce Research

Protein language model for controlled generation. Effective for designing proteins with specific functional properties.

MolFormer
IBM Research

Transformer for molecular property prediction. Strong performance on ADMET (absorption, distribution, metabolism) tasks.

Agent Integration Opportunities

Multi-Model Pipelines: Agents can orchestrate workflows that combine structure prediction (AlphaFold), binding affinity (molecular docking), and ADMET prediction (MolFormer) for end-to-end candidate screening.

Active Learning Loops: Foundation models provide initial predictions, agents select most informative experiments, wet lab validation feeds back to refine models—closing the \( \text{predict} \rightarrow \text{test} \rightarrow \text{learn} \) cycle.

Constraint Satisfaction: Agents can use models as oracles within constraint programming frameworks, ensuring generated molecules satisfy synthetic accessibility, toxicity thresholds, and patent landscape requirements simultaneously.

Critical Gaps

  • Limited training on edge cases: Models trained on known proteins struggle with novel folds and exotic chemistries
  • Synthetic accessibility disconnect: Generated molecules are often impractical to synthesize in lab settings
  • Poor uncertainty quantification: Models don't reliably signal when predictions are unreliable
  • Experimental validation gap: \(\sim\)70% of computationally designed proteins fail experimental validation
