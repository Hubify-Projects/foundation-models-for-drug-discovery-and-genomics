Abstract

Research Question: Which foundation models are producing actionable results in drug discovery and genomics, and how can agents best leverage them?

Foundation models have emerged as transformative tools in computational biology, enabling unprecedented capabilities in protein structure prediction, molecular generation, and genomic sequence analysis. However, the rapid proliferation of these models—spanning protein language models, molecular generative systems, and DNA foundation models—presents significant challenges for practitioners seeking to identify which systems deliver genuine predictive value versus incremental improvements over classical methods.

This research mission systematically catalogs major bio-AI foundation models across three domains (protein modeling, small molecule discovery, and genomics), evaluates their performance on standardized drug discovery benchmarks, and proposes agent workflow architectures for optimal model utilization. Through comprehensive literature review and benchmark analysis, we identify models demonstrating measurable improvements in binding affinity prediction, de novo molecule generation, and variant effect prediction. We conclude with practical integration strategies enabling AI agents to autonomously select and chain foundation models for multi-stage drug discovery pipelines.

Methodology: Phase 1: Comprehensive catalog of bio-AI foundation models with architectural analysis. Phase 2: Systematic evaluation on drug discovery benchmarks (binding affinity, ADMET prediction, de novo generation). Phase 3: Design of agent workflow integration patterns with fallback strategies.

1. Introduction

1.1 Motivation

The convergence of deep learning and life sciences has produced a new generation of foundation models trained on massive biological datasets—from AlphaFold's 200M+ protein structures to molecular transformers trained on billions of drug-like compounds. These models promise to accelerate drug discovery timelines from years to months, reduce wet-lab experimental costs, and enable computational screening at unprecedented scale.

Yet the practical deployment of these models remains challenging. Researchers face questions of model selection (which architecture for which task?), performance validation (does the model generalize beyond its training distribution?), and integration complexity (how to chain models in multi-step workflows?). For AI agent systems designed to autonomously conduct drug discovery research, these challenges are amplified—agents must not only execute model inference but also evaluate confidence, handle edge cases, and orchestrate complex pipelines without human supervision.

1.2 Scope and Objectives

This research focuses on three core domains where foundation models have demonstrated measurable impact:

  • Protein Modeling: Structure prediction (AlphaFold, ESMFold), function prediction (ProteinBERT), and protein design (ProteinMPNN, RFdiffusion)
  • Small Molecule Discovery: Molecular property prediction (ChemBERTa, MolFormer), generative design (REINVENT, MolGPT), and ADMET prediction
  • Genomics: DNA foundation models (DNABERT, Nucleotide Transformer), variant effect prediction (AlphaMissense), and regulatory element identification

Our objectives are threefold: (1) provide a comprehensive, actionable catalog of current foundation models with transparent performance metrics, (2) identify which models deliver genuine predictive value on drug discovery tasks versus marginal improvements, and (3) design practical agent integration patterns that enable autonomous model selection and workflow orchestration.

1.3 Contribution and Novelty

This work makes the following contributions:

  • Systematic Catalog: A structured taxonomy of 30+ bio-AI foundation models with architectural details, training datasets, inference costs, and API availability
  • Benchmark-Driven Evaluation: Performance analysis on standardized drug discovery benchmarks (PDBbind, ZINC, BindingDB) to distinguish hype from genuine capability
  • Agent Integration Framework: Novel workflow patterns for AI agents to autonomously chain foundation models, including confidence-based routing, ensemble strategies, and fallback mechanisms

Relationship to Prior Work: Our catalog builds upon existing surveys of AI in drug discovery (see Section 2) but focuses specifically on foundation models accessible to agent systems. Unlike prior reviews that emphasize model architectures, we prioritize practical deployment considerations: API availability, inference latency, confidence calibration, and failure modes. The agent integration framework represents a novel contribution—to our knowledge, this is the first systematic treatment of how autonomous agents should orchestrate bio-AI foundation models in multi-stage pipelines.

2. Related Work and Prior Art

2.1 Foundation Models in Life Sciences

The concept of foundation models in biology emerged following the success of language model pre-training in NLP. Key architectural innovations include:

Protein Language Models:

  • ESM-2 (Meta, 2022): Transformer trained on 250M protein sequences, demonstrating that unsupervised pre-training captures structural and functional information [Rives et al., 2021; Lin et al., 2022]
  • ProteinBERT (Nadav Brandes, 2022): BERT-style model for protein function prediction, trained on GO annotations [Brandes et al., 2022]
  • AlphaFold2 (DeepMind, 2021): While not strictly a foundation model, AF2's architecture and training on PDB+MSA data established the paradigm of structure prediction from sequence [Jumper et al., 2021]

Molecular Foundation Models:

  • ChemBERTa (Deepchem, 2020): RoBERTa pre-trained on SMILES strings from PubChem, demonstrating transfer learning for molecular property prediction [Chithrananda et al., 2020]
  • MolFormer (IBM, 2022): Large-scale transformer (≈1B parameters) trained on 1.1B molecules, showing improved performance on MoleculeNet benchmarks [Ross et al., 2022]
  • Uni-Mol (DP Technology, 2023): 3D molecular pre-training using conformer ensembles [Zhou et al., 2023]

DNA Foundation Models:

  • DNABERT (Zhang et al., 2021): BERT adapted for genomic sequences with k-mer tokenization [Ji et al., 2021]
  • Nucleotide Transformer (InstaDeep, 2023): Multi-species genome foundation model trained on 3,000+ genomes [Dalla-Torre et al., 2023]
  • Enformer (DeepMind, 2021): Transformer-based gene expression prediction from DNA sequence [Avsec et al., 2021]

2.2 Drug Discovery Benchmarks

Standardized benchmarks are critical for evaluating foundation model performance beyond cherry-picked examples. We reference the following established benchmarks throughout this work:

  • MoleculeNet [Wu et al., 2018]: Suite of molecular property prediction tasks (BACE, HIV, BBBP, Tox21, etc.)
  • PDBbind [Wang et al., 2004]: Binding affinity dataset for protein-ligand complexes
  • BindingDB [Gilson et al., 2016]: Database of measured binding affinities for drug-like molecules
  • ZINC [Irwin et al., 2012]: Commercially available compounds for virtual screening
  • GuacaMol [Brown et al., 2019]: Benchmark for generative molecular design

2.3 AI Agents in Scientific Discovery

The use of autonomous agents for scientific research is an emerging area. Prior work includes:

  • Coscientist (CMU, 2023): LLM-based agent for autonomous chemistry experiments [Boiko et al., 2023]
  • ChemCrow (EPFL, 2023): LangChain-based agent for chemical synthesis planning [Bran et al., 2023]
  • Scientific Discovery with LLMs (Microsoft, 2023): Framework for hypothesis generation and literature mining [Lu et al., 2023]

Our work extends these efforts by focusing specifically on how agents should select and orchestrate foundation models—a critical gap in current agent architectures.

2.4 What This Work Adds

While excellent surveys of AI in drug discovery exist [Schneider et al., 2020; Jiménez-Luna et al., 2021; Vamathevan et al., 2019], they predate the foundation model era or focus on model architectures rather than deployment. Our contribution is:

  • Agent-Centric Perspective: We evaluate models through the lens of autonomous agent integration—API availability, inference cost, confidence calibration, failure modes
  • Actionable Catalog: Beyond listing models, we provide deployment-ready information: endpoints, cost per query, latency, error handling strategies
  • Integration Patterns: Novel workflow architectures for model chaining, ensemble methods, and confidence-based routing

3. Methodology

3.1 Phase 1: Model Catalog Construction

We systematically cataloged foundation models using the following inclusion criteria:

  • Open Access: Model weights publicly available or accessible via API
  • Validated Performance: Published benchmark results on standard datasets
  • Active Maintenance: Updated within the last 24 months (as of December 2024)
  • Relevant to Drug Discovery: Applicable to at least one of: target identification, hit discovery, lead optimization, ADMET prediction, or clinical candidate selection

For each model, we extracted:

  • Architecture (transformer, GNN, CNN, hybrid)
  • Training data (size, source, curation method)
  • Parameter count and computational requirements
  • API availability and pricing (if commercial)
  • Benchmark performance on relevant tasks
  • Known limitations and failure modes

3.2 Phase 2: Benchmark Evaluation

To validate catalog entries, we conducted benchmark evaluations across three task categories:

Binding Affinity Prediction:

  • Dataset: PDBbind refined set (4,852 complexes)
  • Metric: Pearson correlation (ρ) and RMSE of predicted vs. experimental pKd/pKi
  • Baseline: Classical scoring functions (AutoDock Vina, Glide SP)

Molecular Property Prediction:

  • Datasets: MoleculeNet (BACE, BBBP, HIV, Tox21)
  • Metric: ROC-AUC for binary classification, RMSE for regression
  • Baseline: Cheminformatics fingerprints + Random Forest

De Novo Molecular Generation:

  • Benchmark: GuacaMol distribution learning tasks
  • Metrics: Validity, uniqueness, novelty, KL divergence vs. training set
  • Baseline: Graph-based models (GCPN, MolGAN)

Important Note: All benchmark results reported in Section 4 are drawn from published literature or official model documentation. We did not conduct independent re-implementations or evaluations. Where multiple benchmark results exist for the same model, we report ranges and cite sources. This approach prioritizes reproducibility while acknowledging that benchmark performance can vary with implementation details, hyperparameters, and evaluation protocols.

3.3 Phase 3: Agent Integration Design

We designed agent workflow patterns by analyzing common drug discovery pipelines and identifying decision points where model selection is critical. Our framework addresses:

  • Model Selection: How agents choose between alternative models for the same task
  • Confidence Calibration: Interpreting model outputs to assess reliability
  • Fallback Strategies: Handling model failures or low-confidence predictions
  • Pipeline Orchestration: Chaining models in multi-step workflows
  • Cost Optimization: Balancing performance vs. computational expense

We propose three core integration patterns (detailed in Section 5): Sequential Pipeline (model outputs feed subsequent stages), Ensemble Routing (multiple models vote on predictions), and Confidence-Based Fallback (escalate to more expensive models when confidence is low).

4. Results: Foundation Model Catalog

The following catalog represents the state of bio-AI foundation models as of December 2024. Performance metrics are drawn from published papers and official documentation. Models are grouped by domain with focus on those accessible to agent systems (API or open weights).

4.1 Protein Foundation Models

ESM-2 (Meta AI)

650M–15B params Open Weights Hugging Face

Evolutionary Scale Modeling 2 is a protein language model trained on 250M protein sequences from UniRef. Pre-training objective is masked language modeling over amino acid sequences. ESM-2 demonstrates zero-shot structure prediction capabilities competitive with AlphaFold2 on short sequences (≤512 residues), and serves as a strong feature extractor for downstream tasks.

Benchmark Performance:

  • CASP14 structure prediction: 70.1 TM-score (vs. AF2: 87.0) [Lin et al., 2022]
  • Secondary structure prediction: 82.3% 3-state accuracy on CB513 [Lin et al., 2022]
  • Contact prediction: 0.69 precision @ L/5 for long-range contacts [Lin et al., 2022]

Agent Integration Notes: ESM-2 is ideal for rapid structure prediction (seconds vs. minutes for AF2) and feature extraction for binding site identification. Use ESMFold (structure prediction head on ESM-2) for fast screening; escalate to AlphaFold2 for high-confidence structures.

AlphaFold2 / AlphaFold3 (DeepMind)

~93M params API Only (AF3) Open Weights (AF2)

AlphaFold2 revolutionized protein structure prediction, achieving near-experimental accuracy on CASP14. AF3 (2024) extends to protein-ligand, protein-DNA, and multi-chain complexes. Architecture combines MSA processing with structure module operating in 3D space.

Benchmark Performance:

  • CASP14 median TM-score: 0.87 (experimental accuracy: ~0.9) [Jumper et al., 2021]
  • AF3 protein-ligand: 76% success rate (RMSD ≤2Å) vs. 52% for AF2 + docking [Abramson et al., 2024]
  • Median per-residue confidence (pLDDT): 87.3 on PDB validation set [Jumper et al., 2021]

Agent Integration Notes: AF2 is the gold standard for structure prediction but has 5-10 minute runtime for typical proteins. AF3's API has rate limits and cost constraints. Strategy: Use ESMFold for screening, AF2 for refinement, AF3 for critical protein-ligand predictions.

ProteinMPNN (UW / Baker Lab)

~1M params Open Source GitHub

Message-passing neural network for inverse protein folding (structure → sequence). Trained on PDB structures to predict sequences that fold into target backbones. Achieves 52.4% native sequence recovery on CATH 4.2 test set—significantly higher than classical design methods (~35%) [Dauparas et al., 2022].

Agent Integration Notes: Use ProteinMPNN for de novo protein binder design or stabilizing mutants. Pair with ESMFold for rapid design-predict-validate cycles. Low computational cost enables large-scale screening of design candidates.

RFdiffusion (UW / Baker Lab)

~50M params Open Source GitHub

Diffusion model for protein backbone generation. Trained on PDB structures to generate diverse, designable protein folds. Can condition on motifs (e.g. binding sites) or symmetry constraints. Experimental validation shows 55% success rate for binder design (vs. ~15% for prior methods) [Watson et al., 2023].

Agent Integration Notes: RFdiffusion enables truly generative protein design. Use for: (1) binder generation (condition on target epitope), (2) enzyme scaffolding (condition on active site), (3) de novo fold exploration. Pair with ProteinMPNN for sequence design and AF2 for validation.

4.2 Small Molecule Foundation Models

ChemBERTa (Deepchem)

~100M params Open Weights Hugging Face

RoBERTa-based model pre-trained on 77M SMILES strings from PubChem and ZINC. Masked language modeling over SMILES tokens. Serves as strong baseline for molecular property prediction via fine-tuning.

Benchmark Performance (MoleculeNet):

  • BACE (binding affinity): 0.855 ROC-AUC [Chithrananda et al., 2020]
  • BBBP (blood-brain barrier): 0.912 ROC-AUC [Chithrananda et al., 2020]
  • HIV inhibition: 0.776 ROC-AUC [Chithrananda et al., 2020]

Agent Integration Notes: ChemBERTa provides strong baseline for property prediction with minimal fine-tuning data (~100 examples). Use for rapid feasibility assessment of molecular series. Limitations: SMILES-only (no 3D information); sensitive to SMILES canonicalization.

Uni-Mol (DP Technology)

~50M params Open Weights GitHub

3D molecular pre-training on 209M conformers from PubChem3D. Transformer architecture operating on atomic coordinates and features. Pre-training tasks: coordinate denoising, distance/angle prediction, 2D-3D consistency.

Benchmark Performance:

  • QM9 property prediction: MAE 0.012 eV (HOMO-LUMO gap) [Zhou et al., 2023]
  • PDBbind binding affinity: Pearson ρ = 0.82 (vs. 0.76 for 2D models) [Zhou et al., 2023]
  • PCQM4M-LSC quantum chemistry: MAE 0.0723 [Zhou et al., 2023]

Agent Integration Notes: Uni-Mol's 3D awareness makes it superior for tasks where conformation matters (binding affinity, reaction prediction). Trade-off: requires conformer generation (adds latency). Use when geometric features are critical; fall back to ChemBERTa for pure property prediction.

MolFormer (IBM Research)

~1B params Open Weights Hugging Face

Large-scale transformer pre-trained on 1.1B molecules from ZINC, PubChem, and Enamine REAL. Achieves state-of-the-art on multiple MoleculeNet tasks via scale alone (minimal architectural novelty vs. ChemBERTa).

Benchmark Performance:

  • MoleculeNet average: 0.807 ROC-AUC across 11 tasks [Ross et al., 2022]
  • Outperforms ChemBERTa by 2-5% on most tasks [Ross et al., 2022]
  • Low-data regime: 15% better than GNNs with ≤100 training examples [Ross et al., 2022]

Agent Integration Notes: MolFormer is the "bigger is better" approach to molecular modeling. Use when data is limited or when marginal accuracy gains justify increased inference cost. Not necessary for well-studied targets with abundant data.

REINVENT (AstraZeneca)

~10M params Open Source GitHub

LSTM-based generative model for SMILES, trained with reinforcement learning to optimize multi-objective scoring functions. Widely used in industry for lead optimization campaigns.

Benchmark Performance:

  • GuacaMol: 0.89 validity, 0.97 uniqueness, 0.85 novelty [Blaschke et al., 2020]
  • Goal-directed generation: Comparable to graph-based models on molecular optimization tasks [Blaschke et al., 2020]
  • Practical validation: Multiple compounds generated by REINVENT are in preclinical development [Blaschke et al., 2020]

Agent Integration Notes: REINVENT excels at constrained optimization (e.g. improve potency while maintaining ADMET properties). Use for lead optimization, not hit discovery. Requires careful reward function design—agents should implement multi-objective Pareto optimization rather than single-metric maximization.

4.3 DNA and Genomics Foundation Models

Nucleotide Transformer (InstaDeep)

50M–2.5B params Open Weights Hugging Face

Multi-species genome foundation model trained on 3,202 genomes (850GB of DNA sequences). Architecture is standard transformer with positional embeddings for up to 6kb context. Pre-training: masked nucleotide prediction. Enables cross-species transfer learning for regulatory element prediction.

Benchmark Performance:

  • Promoter detection (human): 0.94 ROC-AUC [Dalla-Torre et al., 2023]
  • Enhancer prediction: 0.87 ROC-AUC [Dalla-Torre et al., 2023]
  • Chromatin accessibility: 0.81 Spearman correlation with DNase-seq [Dalla-Torre et al., 2023]
  • Zero-shot transfer: 10-15% improvement over single-species models on rare organisms [Dalla-Torre et al., 2023]

Agent Integration Notes: Use Nucleotide Transformer for genomic feature extraction in drug target validation. Example workflow: (1) identify disease-associated variants, (2) use NT to predict regulatory impact, (3) prioritize targets with high predicted expression changes. Limitation: 6kb context insufficient for long-range interactions (use Enformer for gene expression prediction).
